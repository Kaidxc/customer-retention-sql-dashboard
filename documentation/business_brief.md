# Business Brief

## Stakeholder

Merchandising, buying, commercial planning or ecommerce trading team at a UK online retailer.

## Business situation

The retailer has a broad product catalogue and two years of order-line history. The business needs to understand what product types exist after cleaning, what customer purchases look like, which products drive sales, which products create volume, where sales are concentrated geographically, which products appear to be slowing down, and which stable products should be considered in next-quarter planning.

## Decision supported

Which products should be protected, promoted, reviewed, cleared, or forecast for the next quarter?

## Analytical objective

Use historical transaction data to:

1. Measure product-level quantity, revenue, order count and customer reach.
2. Summarise customer purchase behaviour and order-level basket size.
3. Identify top products by revenue and by units sold.
4. Quantify how concentrated revenue is across the product catalogue.
5. Summarise country/region sales context.
6. Flag products with meaningful historical revenue but weak recent sales.
7. Produce a simple next-quarter forecast baseline for stable high-revenue products.
8. Backtest the forecast baseline so the business understands planning risk.
9. Document a Power BI-ready data model and reporting structure.

## Business recommendation

Protect availability for high-revenue products, use high-unit lower-revenue products for basket-building, review slow-moving products before discounting or delisting, and use the product forecast as a planning baseline rather than a final buying decision. Product actions should be evaluated with clear metrics such as revenue, units, stock availability, sell-through rate, margin and forecast error once those data fields are available.
